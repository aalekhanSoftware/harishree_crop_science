What is Imidacloprid 70% WG?

Imidacloprid 70% WG (EXTRA MIDA 70) is a neonicotinoid systemic insecticide in water-dispersible granule form that controls whiteflies, aphids, jassids, thrips, and other sucking pests in cotton, chilli, paddy, sugarcane, citrus, and vegetables. It absorbs rapidly into plant tissue for long residual protection. Recommended dose: 7 gm per pump.

How does Imidacloprid 70% WG work?

Neonicotinoid mode of action — Imidacloprid disrupts nicotinic receptors for systemic, contact, and stomach pest control.

  1. 1

    Rapid foliar absorption

    On application, EXTRA MIDA 70 WG disperses in water and is absorbed through the leaf cuticle, entering the plant vascular system within hours.

  2. 2

    Contact action on pest body

    Imidacloprid affects pests on direct contact, rapidly disrupting nerve function and stopping sap-sucking damage on treated foliage.

  3. 3

    Stomach action via ingestion

    Pests feeding on treated plant tissue ingest imidacloprid, leading to feeding cessation and mortality even when not directly sprayed.

  4. 4

    Systemic movement within plant

    The active ingredient translocates through xylem and phloem, reaching new shoots and leaves that were not directly sprayed.

  5. 5

    Nervous system disruption

    Imidacloprid binds to nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (IRAC Group 4A), causing continuous nerve stimulation, paralysis, and death.

  6. 6

    Long lasting protection

    Combined surface deposit and internal plant concentration continue working between sprays, protecting crops from newly arriving sucking pests.

Imidacloprid 70% WG mode of action

Deep dive into neonicotinoid chemistry and how EXTRA MIDA 70 protects crops.

Neonicotinoid chemistry

Imidacloprid (IRAC Group 4A) mimics nicotine, binding irreversibly to nicotinic acetylcholine receptors and disrupting nerve signal transmission in target pests.

Systemic movement

After foliar uptake, imidacloprid translocates through xylem and phloem, reaching unsprayed new growth and protecting the plant from inside out.

Pest paralysis mechanism

Continuous nerve stimulation causes feeding cessation within hours, followed by paralysis and mortality — even in pests feeding on treated tissue they did not directly contact.

Residual protection

Neonicotinoid chemistry provides extended field persistence. Combined surface deposit and internal plant concentration protect crops between spray intervals.
